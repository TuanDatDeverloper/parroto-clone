Frequently Asked Questions

Questions about learning English on Parroto? Find answers to the common ones below.

Dictation means listening to audio or video and typing back exactly what you hear, word for word — the way to train deep listening. Shadowing means listening to a native speaker and repeating immediately, to sharpen your pronunciation, intonation and rhythm. Parroto combines both on real-world video, and that is the core: around it sit contextual vocabulary, 1-on-1 community speaking practice, and IELTS/TOEIC preparation.

When you shadow, AI analyses your recording in real time and points out exactly which sound, which stress or which linking is off compared with a native speaker, so you can fix it while you practise. Repeating straight after a native speaker trains your mouth to form the right sounds and your ear to recognise correct pronunciation. When you want to use it for real, you can get matched 1-on-1 for speaking practice with other learners in the community.

Yes. Parroto has lessons from A1 all the way to C2, and lets you slow the playback down so beginners can hear every word clearly before repeating it. Content is organised by difficulty, so you start at your current ability and step up as your skills improve.

15–20 minutes a day, every day, beats cramming. Consistency matters more than duration — short regular sessions outperform long occasional ones. Parroto's lessons are deliberately short so the habit survives a busy schedule.

Lessons span film clips, cartoons, TV shows, news, songs, everyday conversation, business and travel English, and IELTS, TOEIC and TOEFL preparation. Everything is hand-picked and updated regularly, so you always practise on real English in a topic you actually care about.

On top of that there is IELTS/TOEIC preparation with instantly scored mock tests — something most vocabulary apps simply don't have.
